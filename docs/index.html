The Ladder

Smart tools that help people get smarter. They make guesses, and the real world tells us if they were right. Tools that are not alive, raising the person who uses them. Narrow, goal-free tools whose predictions are sealed in advance and scored against reality and stronger baselines.

The big idea: we don't build a robot to be smart for us. We build helpers that make us smarter, one step at a time, like climbing a ladder. “The bootstrap is a set of tools, not a creature. Each is superhuman at one job: designing, predicting, checking. None has goals of its own, and none acts unless a person asks.” “The bootstrap is a set of tools, not a creature. II

Sky: guessing space weatherSky: calling space weatherSky: geomagnetic Kp forecasting

The Sun is always blowing a wind out into space. A spacecraft far away feels it first, about an hour before it reaches Earth. Every 3 hours, our helper guesses how much that wind will shake Earth's magnetic field. The scale goes from 0 (calm) to 9 (a huge storm with bright northern lights). Scientists at NOAA, America's weather and space-weather agency, make their guess too. Then the real number comes in, and we see who was closest.

Right now, a spacecraft 1.5 million km away is measuring the solar wind before it reaches Earth. Every three hours the Predictor seals a guess for how stirred-up Earth's magnetic field will get (the Kp scale, 0 calm to 9 extreme storm). Then NOAA's measurement scores it. Its rivals are NOAA's own forecasters, and a lazy guess that just repeats the last value.

Real-time solar wind (plasma and IMF in GSM) from the active L1 spacecraft via NOAA SWPC RTSW. For each 3-hour UT block, a Kp forecast is sealed before the block starts. It blends the Newell et al. (2008) coupling relation, averaged over the last 60 minutes, with persistence. The SWPC forecast and persistence are sealed at the same moment as baselines. Everything is scored by absolute error against SWPC's preliminary planetary Kp.

III

Desk: the paper helicopterDesk: the paper helicopterDesk: rotor descent forecasting

You can do this one at home! All you need is paper, scissors, a paper clip and a phone timer. The helper picks a shape. You guess first: how many seconds will it take to fall? Then the helper's guess gets locked in too. Drop it, time it, and see who was closer. Every time you play, the helper learns and gets a little better.

A test you can run at your desk with paper, scissors, a paper clip and your phone's stopwatch. The Designer picks the shape. You guess first, then the Predictor seals its own guess. You drop it and the Checker scores you both. After every drop the tool learns, so its next guess should get closer. That's the ladder you can watch.

A physical forecasting task with a human comparison arm. The model is steady descent: mg = ½ρCdAv² with blade area A, and t = h/v + v/2g. It has one free parameter, ln Cd, with a normal prior that each round's implied Cd updates. The human forecast is sealed before the model's output is shown.
